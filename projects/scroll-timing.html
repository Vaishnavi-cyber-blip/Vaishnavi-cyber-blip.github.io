Breaking the Scroll at the Right Moment

Apps that interrupt doom-scrolling exist. We ask when an interruption actually helps people stop scrolling on autopilot, and whether a watch vibration or an on-screen pause works better.

Research questions

RQ1When?

At which moments does an interruption help someone stop scrolling on autopilot?

RQ2Through which sense?

Does a watch vibration or an on-screen pause work better?

Two ways to interrupt

The two conditions we plan to compare:

A haptic nudge on the wrist. It reaches the person without covering what they are looking at, so it could be gentler, but it may also be easy to ignore.

A short pause shown on the phone. It is hard to miss because it sits where attention already is, but it may feel more intrusive.

These are the trade-offs we expect to look at, not results.

Why it matters

A well-timed nudge can help a person regain control, while a badly timed one is ignored or annoying. The study applies the lab's interest in intervention timing to everyday attention, and it fits our review of adaptive wearables, where timing and modality are two of the main ways interventions differ.
